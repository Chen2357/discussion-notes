§I Warm-Up

(Warm-Up) How to count to infinity in 10 minutes?

Solution. There are many ways to do this, one of them is to use geometric series. When we start our timer,

  • we can spend the first 5 minutes just to count 1, we have 5 minutes remaining;
  • spend the next 5/2 minutes to count 2, we have 5/2 minutes remaining;
  • spend the next 5/4 minutes to count 3, we have 5/4 minutes remaining;
  • spend the next 5/8 minutes to count 4, we have 5/8 minutes remaining;
  • and so on.

You can see that no matter how high we count, we will always have some time remaining, and we can just spend half of the remaining time to count the next integer. By the time our timer runs out, we will have counted infinitely many integers.

This example illustrates that

5+52+54+58+⋯=10.

§II Geometric Series

A geometric series is a series

𝑠=𝑎1+𝑎2+𝑎3+⋯

whose terms are related by a common ratio 𝑟:

𝑎1↦×𝑟𝑎2↦×𝑟𝑎3↦×𝑟𝑎4↦×𝑟⋯.

We can compare this series with the scaled version

𝑟𝑠=𝑎2+𝑎3+𝑎4+⋯

to find that their difference is

𝑠−𝑟𝑠=𝑎1.

Therefore, we can solve for 𝑠 to get

𝑠=𝑎11−𝑟,

or can we?

One thing I want to bring your attention to is that the the above derivation only works when |𝑟|<1 (so the boxes in Figure 1 do not grow unboundedly). The full geometric series formula states that

𝑎1+𝑎2+𝑎3+⋯={𝑎11−𝑟if|𝑟|<1,divergesif|𝑟|≥1.

Applying this formula to the warm-up question, we have that because 𝑟=12 and |𝑟|=12<1,

5+52+54+58+⋯=51−12=10.

It is very important for you to write out that |𝑟|<1 before applying the geometric series formula.

Also, it is not important that you index the terms exactly in the way I did. You can see from the concrete example that we are just taking the first term and dividing it by 1−𝑟.

(Exercise) Determine whether the following series converge, if they do, find their sums.

  1. 12+14+18+116+132+⋯
  2. ∑𝑛=2∞(−2)𝑛3𝑛+1
  3. ∑𝑛=1∞3(−2𝑛)

Solution.

  1. This is a geometric series with ratio 1/2. Because |1/2|<1, it converges and its sum is the first term divided by 1−12, which is

    121−12=1.

  2. We can rewrite the series as

    (−2)233+(−2)334+(−2)435+⋯

    This makes it clear that this is a geometric series with first term (−2)233 and common ratio −23. Because |−23|<1, the series converges and its sum is the first term divided by 1−(−23), which is

    (−2)2331−(−23)=42753=445.

  3. Recall that

    3(−2𝑛)=(13)2𝑛=(132)𝑛,

    so the series can be written as

    (132)+(132)2+(132)3+⋯.

    This is a geometric series with first term 132 and common ratio 132<1, so it converges and its sum

    1321−132=191−19=1989=18.

§III Integral and Direct Comparison Test

Both the integral test and the direct comparison test are based on the following principle.

(Principle of nonnegative series) If the series

𝑎1+𝑎2+𝑎3+𝑎4+⋯

consists only of nonnegative terms (that is 𝑎𝑛≥0 for all 𝑛), then it either converges to a finite nonnegative number or diverges to infinity. The limit is always well-defined!

This is due to the monotone convergence theorem and you can think about how they relate to each other. The principle is not necessarily true for series with negative terms, for example 1−1+1−1+⋯ does not have a well-defined limit.

We have a simple characterization of series with nonnegative terms: it converges when there is a finite upper bound, and it diverges when it is infinite (we do not have to worry about divergence in the sense of having ill-defined limit like 1−1+1−1+⋯).

(Integral test) Let 𝑎𝑁,𝑎𝑁+1,𝑎𝑁+2,… be a sequence of nonnegative terms. Suppose that 𝑎𝑛=𝑓(𝑛), where 𝑓(𝑥) is a nonnegative and decreasing function of 𝑥 for all 𝑥≥𝑁. Then, we have the inequality

∫𝑁∞𝑓(𝑥) 𝑑𝑥≤∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑎𝑛≤𝑎𝑁+∫𝑁∞𝑓(𝑥) 𝑑𝑥

due to the following figures.

In particular,

  • if ∫𝑁∞𝑓(𝑥) 𝑑𝑥=𝐿 is finite, then ∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑎𝑛≤𝑎𝑁+𝐿 is also finite by the upper bound;
  • if ∫𝑁∞𝑓(𝑥) 𝑑𝑥=∞, then ∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑎𝑛=∞ by the lower bound.

The direct comparison test is an intuitive result.

(Direct comparison test) Let ∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑎𝑛 and ∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑏𝑛 be two series with 0≤𝑎𝑛≤𝑏𝑛. Then,

0≤∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑎𝑛≤∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑏𝑛.

In particular,

  • if ∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑏𝑛 is finite, then ∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑎𝑛 is also finite by the upper bound;
  • if ∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑎𝑛=∞, then ∑𝑛=𝑁∞𝑏𝑛=∞ by the lower bound.

In the following exercise, we might want to use the comparison test in conjunction with the integral test: we can compare a complicated series with a simpler one that yields a simple integral when we apply the integral test.

(Exercise) Determine whether the following series converge or diverge. (For the sake of exercise, try to use the integral test instead of 𝑝-series test.)

  1. 1+14+19+116+125+⋯
  2. ∑𝑛=1∞2+cos𝑛𝑛 (hint: −1≤cos𝑥≤1)
  3. ∑𝑛=1∞2+cos𝑛𝑛2

  4. ∑𝑛=1∞1(𝑛−4)2+1 (hint: ∫0∞1𝑥2+1 𝑑𝑥=𝜋2)

  5. Bonus: For the convergent series above, find a lower bound and an upper bound for their sums.

Solution.

  1. This series can be written as

    ∑𝑛=1∞1𝑛2.

    Notice that 1𝑥2 is nonnegative and decreasing for 𝑥≥1, so it converges if and only if the integral

    ∫1∞1𝑥2 𝑑𝑥

    converges. We compute that

    ∫1∞1𝑥2 𝑑𝑥=[−1𝑥]1∞=1,

    so the series converges.

  2. Notice that this series is nonnegative because 2+cos𝑛≥1≥0, so we can apply the direct comparison test. Because 1≤2+cos𝑛≤3, we have

    1𝑛≤2+cos𝑛𝑛≤3𝑛.

    Since ∑𝑛=1∞1𝑛=∞ by the integral test:

    ∫1∞1𝑥 𝑑𝑥=[ln𝑥]1∞=∞,

    we also have that ∑𝑛=1∞2+cos𝑛𝑛=∞ diverges.

  3. Still using 1≤2+cos𝑛≤3, we have

    1𝑛2≤2+cos𝑛𝑛2≤3𝑛2.

    As shown in part 1, ∑𝑛=1∞1𝑛2 converges, so ∑𝑛=1∞3𝑛2 converges as well. By the direct comparison test, we have that

    ∑𝑛=1∞2+cos𝑛𝑛2≤∑𝑛=1∞3𝑛2<∞

    converges.

  4. It is very tempting to apply the integral test, but notice that the terms in

    ∑𝑛=1∞1(𝑛−4)2+1=19+1+14+1+11+1+10+1+11+1+14+1+19+1+⋯

    are not decreasing (also clear from Figure 4), so any interpolating function would also not be decreasing. The good news is that we can extract the first three terms

    19+1+14+1+11+1=110+15+12=810,

    and apply the integral test to the rest of the series

    10+1+11+1+14+1+19+1+⋯=∑𝑛=0∞1𝑛2+1.

    Since 1𝑥2+1 is nonnegative and decreasing for 𝑥≥0, we have

    ∫0∞1𝑥2+1 𝑑𝑥≤∑𝑛=0∞1𝑛2+1≤10+1+∫0∞1𝑥2+1 𝑑𝑥.

    Using the hint, this inequality becomes

    𝜋2≤∑𝑛=0∞1𝑛2+1≤1+𝜋2.

    Therefore, the original series converges and its sum is between

    810+𝜋2and810+1+𝜋2.

  5. For the series in part 1, we have

    1=∫1∞1𝑥2 𝑑𝑥≤∑𝑛=1∞1𝑛2≤1+∫1∞1𝑥2 𝑑𝑥=2.

    For the series in part 3, we have

    1≤∑𝑛=1∞2+cos𝑛𝑛2≤3∑𝑛=1∞1𝑛2≤6.

    The range 1 to 6 is quite a loose bound, we can get better bounds by extracting initial terms and applying the integral test to the rest of the series. For example, we can break the series into

    1+14+19+116+∑𝑛=5∞1𝑛2.

    Applying the integral test to the last part, we have

    ∫5∞1𝑥2 𝑑𝑥≤∑𝑛=5∞1𝑛2≤125+∫5∞1𝑥2 𝑑𝑥.

    Calculating the integral, we have

    ∫5∞1𝑥2 𝑑𝑥=[−1𝑥]5∞=15.

    thus, the original series is between

    1+14+19+116+15and1+14+19+116+125+15.

    In decimals, we find that the sum is between 1.623 and 1.664.

    We have already found that the sum of the series in part 4 is between 810+𝜋2≈2.370 and 810+1+𝜋2≈3.371.
