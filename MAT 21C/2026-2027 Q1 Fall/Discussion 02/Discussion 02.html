§I Warm-Up

(Warm-Up) Order the following numbers from least to greatest:

2027,20272,22027,20272027,2027!,ln2027,32027.

Solution. The correct order is

ln2027<2027<20272<22027<32027<2027!<20272027.

Some of my intuition:

  • ln2027<2027 because ln2027 is certainly less than 40 (2027 is less than 𝑒40) and 40 is less than 2027. Or make the plot of ln𝑥 and 𝑥 and we will see that ln𝑥 is always below 𝑥.
  • 2027<20272 because 2027 is less than 2027 and 2027 is less than 20272.
  • 20272<22027 because exponential grows very fast, 210=1024.
  • 22027<32027 because 2<3.
  • 32027<2027! because they are both products of 2027 numbers, but 32027 is the product of 2027 threes, while 2027! is the product of 1,2,3,…,2027, which is much larger.
  • 2027!<20272027 because 20272027 is the product of 2027 copies of 2027, which is even larger.

§II Limit Comparison Test

Consider the series

∑𝑛=1∞𝑛−ln𝑛𝑛2+𝑛+1.

Intuitively, for very large 𝑛, we want to say that 𝑛 is so large compared to ln𝑛 that the numerator is effectively 𝑛, and similarly, 𝑛2 is so large compared to 𝑛 and 1 that the denominator is effectively 𝑛2. Therefore, we are just looking at the series

∑𝑛=1∞𝑛𝑛2=∑𝑛=1∞1𝑛

which is a divergent 𝑝-series with 𝑝=1.

The limit comparison test is what makes this intuition rigorous. Let us introduce the notation that

𝑎𝑛≪𝑏𝑛iflim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛=0or equivalentlylim𝑛→∞𝑏𝑛𝑎𝑛=∞.

The limit comparison test states that

If 𝑎𝑛≥0 and 𝑏𝑛≥0 are nonnegative terms, then

  1. If lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛=𝑐 and 0<𝑐<∞, then ∑𝑎𝑛 and ∑𝑏𝑛 either both converge or both diverge.

  2. If 𝑎𝑛≪𝑏𝑛, then the convergence of ∑𝑏𝑛 implies the convergence of ∑𝑎𝑛 and the divergence of ∑𝑎𝑛 implies the divergence of ∑𝑏𝑛. It is as if

    ∑𝑎𝑛≤∑𝑏𝑛

    except that technically, this inequality is only true if the we restrict the lower bounds of the series to be sufficiently large.

If we return to our example, we have concluded that we should compare the series to ∑𝑛=1∞1𝑛 and this is exactly what we will do. Rather than using nested fractions, it is the most elegant to multiply 𝑛−ln𝑛𝑛2+𝑛+1 by the inverse of 1𝑛, so we have

lim𝑛→∞𝑛−ln𝑛𝑛2+𝑛+1·𝑛1=lim𝑛→∞𝑛2−𝑛ln𝑛𝑛2+𝑛+1=lim𝑛→∞1−ln𝑛𝑛1+1𝑛+1𝑛2=1

where we used the fact that

lim𝑛→∞ln𝑛𝑛=LHlim𝑛→∞1𝑛1=0.

Before we move on to exercises, let us note that

constant≪ln𝑛≪⋯≪𝑛13≪𝑛12≪𝑛≪𝑛2≪𝑛3≪⋯≪𝑒𝑛≪𝑛!≪𝑛𝑛.

Remembering this will help us identify the fastest growing terms in the numerator and denominator of a series.

(Exercise) Determine whether the following series converge or diverge.

  1. ∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛+53𝑛
  2. ∑𝑛=2∞1𝑛𝑛2−1
  3. ∑𝑛=1∞𝑛2𝑒𝑛 (hint: split 𝑛2𝑒𝑛=𝑛2𝑒𝑛/2·1𝑒𝑛/2)

Solution.

  1. The fastest growing term in the numerator is 2𝑛 and the fastest growing term in the denominator is 3𝑛, so we will compare to ∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛3𝑛. We have

    lim𝑛→∞2𝑛+53𝑛·3𝑛2𝑛=lim𝑛→∞(1+52𝑛)=1.

    Since ∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛3𝑛 is a convergent geometric series, we conclude that ∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛+53𝑛 converges.

    This problem can be more easily solved by splitting the series into two parts. This is mistake in problem design on my part. A better problem is perhaps

    ∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛+53𝑛−2𝑛.

  2. For large 𝑛, 1 is negligible compared to 𝑛2, so we will compare to ∑𝑛=2∞1𝑛𝑛2=∑𝑛=2∞1𝑛2. We have

    lim𝑛→∞1𝑛𝑛2−1·𝑛2=lim𝑛→∞𝑛𝑛2−1=lim𝑛→∞11−1𝑛2=1.

    Since ∑𝑛=2∞1𝑛2 is a convergent 𝑝-series with 𝑝=2>1, we conclude that ∑𝑛=2∞1𝑛𝑛2−1 converges.

  3. We expect that 𝑛2≪𝑒𝑛/2, so we will expect

    𝑛2𝑒𝑛=𝑛2𝑒𝑛/2·1𝑒𝑛/2≪1𝑒𝑛/2=(1𝑒1/2)𝑛.

    Explicitly, we need to write

    lim𝑛→∞𝑛2𝑒𝑛·𝑒𝑛/21=lim𝑛→∞𝑛2𝑒𝑛/2=LHlim𝑛→∞4𝑛𝑒𝑛/2=LHlim𝑛→∞8𝑒𝑛/2=0.

    Since ∑𝑛=1∞1𝑒𝑛/2 is a convergent geometric series (|𝑟|=𝑒−1/2<1), we conclude that ∑𝑛=1∞𝑛2𝑒𝑛 converges.

§III Ratio and Root Tests

The ratio and root tests extend our ability to determine convergence and divergence of series that involve products of factorial terms 𝑛! and power terms like 𝑛𝑘, 𝑎𝑛, and 𝑛𝑛.

For series with factorials, the ratio test is essentially the exclusive test. For example, consider

∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛𝑛!.

We will set 𝑎𝑛=2𝑛𝑛! and decide whether

lim𝑛→∞|𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛|

is less than 1 (convergent), greater than 1 (divergent), or equal to 1 (inconclusive). The elegant way to write 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎𝑛 is not through nested fractions but rather multiplying 𝑎𝑛+1 by the inverse of 𝑎𝑛:

𝑎𝑛+11𝑎𝑛=2𝑛+1(𝑛+1)!·𝑛!2𝑛=2𝑛+1.

Thus, we have

lim𝑛→∞|𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛|=lim𝑛→∞2𝑛+1=0<1,

which implies that the series ∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛𝑛! converges.

The root test is effective when the terms are products of power terms. For example, consider

∑𝑛=1∞𝑛7𝑛𝑛.

We can apply the root test by looking at the limit

lim𝑛→∞|𝑎𝑛|1𝑛=lim𝑛→∞𝑛7/𝑛𝑛=lim𝑛→∞(𝑛1/𝑛)7𝑛=0<1.

Similar to the ratio test, a limit of less than 1 implies convergence, a limit greater than 1 implies divergence, and a limit equal to 1 is inconclusive.

An identity that is worth remembering for the root test is that

lim𝑛→∞𝑛1/𝑛=1,

which we used in the previous example.

(Exercise) Determine whether the following series converge or diverge.

  1. ∑𝑛=1∞(2𝑛)!𝑛+2
  2. ∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛𝑒𝑛2

Solution.

  1. We will apply the ratio test. We calculate

    lim𝑛→∞|𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛|=lim𝑛→∞(2(𝑛+1))!𝑛+3·𝑛+2(2𝑛)!=lim𝑛→∞(2𝑛+2)!𝑛+3·𝑛+2(2𝑛)!=lim𝑛→∞(2𝑛+2)(2𝑛+1)𝑛+2𝑛+3=lim𝑛→∞(2𝑛+2)(2𝑛+1)1+2/𝑛1+3/𝑛=∞>1.

    Since the limit is greater than 1, we conclude that the series ∑𝑛=1∞(2𝑛)!𝑛+2 diverges.

  2. Ratio test also works well, but we will apply the root test. We calculate

    lim𝑛→∞|𝑎𝑛|1𝑛=lim𝑛→∞(2𝑛𝑒𝑛2)1𝑛=lim𝑛→∞21/𝑛𝑛1/𝑛𝑒𝑛=0<1.

    Since the limit is less than 1, we conclude that the series ∑𝑛=1∞2𝑛𝑒𝑛2 converges.
